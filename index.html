Question 1 Past Paper 9 marks

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((-10,\,0)\) and \((30,\,0)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\). It is given that the \(y\)-coordinate of \(G\) is \(-15\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). (3 marks)
(b) The straight line \(L\) passes through \(B\) and \(G\). Another straight line \(\ell\) is parallel to \(L\). Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L\) is equal to the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(\ell\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that \(\Gamma\) passes through \(A\).
(i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(L\). (1 mark)
(ii) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). (2 marks)
(iii) Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at another point \(H\). Someone claims that \(\angle GAH < 70°\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) The \(x\)-coordinate of \(G\) is the midpoint of \(A\) and \(B\):
\(\dfrac{-10+30}{2}=10\). So \(G=(10,-15)\).		 1M
The radius of \(C\):
\(r=\sqrt{(-10-10)^2+(0-(-15))^2}=\sqrt{400+225}=\sqrt{625}=25\)		 1M
Equation of \(C\): \(\displaystyle\class{ans}{(x-10)^2+(y+15)^2=625}\)
or equivalently \(x^2+y^2-20x+30y-300=0\)		 1A
(b)(i) \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\). 1M
(b)(ii) Slope of \(L\) through \(B(30,0)\) and \(G(10,-15)\):
\(m_L=\dfrac{-15-0}{10-30}=\dfrac{-15}{-20}=\dfrac{3}{4}\)		 1M
Since \(\Gamma \parallel L\) and \(\Gamma\) passes through \(A(-10,0)\):
\(y-0=\tfrac{3}{4}(x-(-10))\)
\(\class{ans}{3x-4y+30=0}\)		 1A or equivalent
(b)(iii) \(\tan\angle ABG=\dfrac{3}{4}\), so \(\angle ABG\approx36.87°\). 1M
Note that \(\angle BAH=\angle ABG\) (base angles, isosceles \(\triangle ABH\) since \(HA=HB\) as \(H\) lies on \(\Gamma\)) and \(\angle BAG=\angle ABG\) (base angles, since \(GA=GB=r\)).
\(\angle GAH=\angle BAH+\angle BAG=2\angle ABG\approx73.74°\).		 1M
Since \(\angle ABG>35°\), we have \(\angle GAH>70°\).
The claim is disagreed.		 1A f.t.
Total: 9 marks
Question 2 Past Paper 7 marks

Denote the origin by \(O\).

A vertical line which passes through \(B\) cuts the straight line \(y=mx\) at the point \(C\), where \(m\) is a positive constant such that \(AB=BC\). Let \(D\) be a point such that \(ABCD\) is a square. Express the slope of \(OD\) in terms of \(m\). (3 marks)
(b) The coordinates of the points \(M\) and \(N\) are \((6,5)\) and \((10,0)\) respectively. Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be points lying on \(OM\) and \(MN\) respectively while \(R\) and \(S\) be points lying on the \(x\)-axis. If the quadrilateral \(PQRS\) is a square, find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\). (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Let \(B=(b,0)\). Then \(C=(b,\,mb)\), \(A=(mb+b,\,0)=(b(m+1),0)\), and \(D=(mb+b,\,mb)\). 1M for any one
Slope of \(OD=\dfrac{mb-0}{mb+b-0}=\dfrac{mb}{b(m+1)}=\class{ans}{\dfrac{m}{m+1}}\) 1M, 1A
(b) Slope of \(OM=\dfrac{5-0}{6-0}=\dfrac{5}{6}\).
By part (a), slope of \(OQ=\dfrac{5/6}{5/6+1}=\dfrac{5/6}{11/6}=\dfrac{5}{11}\).		 1M using result of (a)
Equation of line through \(O\) and \(Q\): \(y=\dfrac{5}{11}x\).
Equation of line \(MN\): \(y-0=\dfrac{5-0}{6-10}(x-10)\Rightarrow y=\dfrac{-5}{4}x+\dfrac{25}{2}\)		 1M
Solving \(y=\tfrac{5}{11}x\) and \(y=\tfrac{-5}{4}x+\tfrac{25}{2}\) simultaneously, the coordinates of \(Q\) are \(\left(\dfrac{22}{3},\dfrac{10}{3}\right)\).
\(x\)-coordinate of \(P = \dfrac{22}{3} - \dfrac{10}{3} = \class{ans}{4}\) 1M, 1A

Question 3 Past Paper 7 marks

The straight line \(L\) cuts the positive \(x\)-axis and the positive \(y\)-axis at the points \(M\) and \(N\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(MP=NP\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that the equation of \(\Gamma\) is \(3x-2y-30=0\).

(a) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(MN\). (1 mark)
(b) Find the equation of \(L\). (3 marks)
(c) Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(L\) and the \(x\)-axis at the points \(Q\) and \(R\) respectively. Find the area of the quadrilateral \(ONQR\), where \(O\) is the origin. (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\). 1M
(b) Let \(M=(a,0)\) and \(N=(0,b)\). The slope of \(L\) is \(\dfrac{-b}{a}\).
Since \(\Gamma\perp L\) (by (a)): \(\left(\dfrac{-b}{a}\right)\!\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)=-1\), so \(2a=3b\).		 1M
The midpoint of \(MN\) is \(\left(\dfrac{a}{2},\dfrac{b}{2}\right)\), which lies on \(\Gamma\):
\(3\!\left(\dfrac{a}{2}\right)-2\!\left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)-30=0\Rightarrow a=36,\;b=24\).		 1M
Equation of \(L\): \(y-0=\dfrac{-24}{36}(x-36)\Rightarrow\class{ans}{2x+3y-72=0}\) 1A or equivalent
(c) \(N=(0,24)\), \(Q\) = intersection of \(\Gamma\) and \(L\):
Solving \(3x-2y=30\) and \(2x+3y=72\) gives \(Q=(18,12)\).
\(R\) = \(x\)-intercept of \(\Gamma\): set \(y=0\Rightarrow x=10\). So \(R=(10,0)\).		 1M
Area of \(ONQR\) (trapezoid \(ON\) on \(y\)-axis, \(OR\) on \(x\)-axis):
\(=\dfrac{(24)(18)}{2}+\dfrac{(10)(12)}{2}=216+60=\class{ans}{276}\)		 1M, 1A

Question 4 Past Paper 7 marks

The straight line \(L\) passes through the point \((-10,\,11)\). The slope of \(L\) is \(-2\).

(a) Find the equation of \(L\). (2 marks)
(b) The coordinates of the points \(H\) and \(K\) are \((2,5)\) and \((-4,-7)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a point lying on \(L\) such that \(P\) is equidistant from \(H\) and \(K\).
(i) Find the coordinates of \(P\). (2 marks)
(ii) Let \(Q\) be a point such that \(HPKQ\) is a quadrilateral where \(HP=HQ=KQ\). Find the area of the quadrilateral \(HPKQ\). (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) \(-2=\dfrac{y-11}{x-(-10)}\Rightarrow\class{ans}{2x+y+9=0}\) 1M, 1A
(b)(i) \(L: y=-2x-9\). Let \(P=(p,\,-2p-9)\).
Setting \(PH=PK\):
\(\sqrt{(p-2)^2+(-2p-14)^2}=\sqrt{(p+4)^2+(-2p-2)^2}\)
\((p-2)^2+(-2p-14)^2=(p+4)^2+(-2p-2)^2\)
Expanding: \(p+5=0\Rightarrow p=-5\).		 1M
\(\therefore P=(-5,\,-2(-5)-9)=\class{ans}{(-5,\,1)}\) 1A
(b)(ii) \(HP=\sqrt{(2-(-5))^2+(5-1)^2}=\sqrt{49+16}=\sqrt{65}\).
\(PK=\sqrt{(-5-(-4))^2+(1-(-7))^2}=\sqrt{1+64}=\sqrt{65}=HP\).
So \(P\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(HK\), confirming \(Q\) is the reflection of \(P\) through \(HK\) midpoint.		 1M
Since \(HQ=KQ=HP=\sqrt{65}\), point \(Q\) also lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(HK\).
Midpoint of \(HK=(-1,-1)\), slope of \(HK=2\), slope of perp. bisector \(=-\tfrac{1}{2}\).
Perp. bisector: \(y+1=-\tfrac{1}{2}(x+1)\Rightarrow y=-\tfrac{x}{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}\).
Solving \((x-2)^2+(-\tfrac{x}{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}-5)^2=65\):
\(x^2+2x-15=0\Rightarrow(x+5)(x-3)=0\Rightarrow x=-5\) (giving \(P\)) or \(x=3\).
\(Q=\left(3,\,-3\right)\).		 1M
Since \(HP=PK=KQ=QH=\sqrt{65}\), \(HPKQ\) is a rhombus.
Diagonals: \(HK=\sqrt{(2+4)^2+(5+7)^2}=\sqrt{180}=6\sqrt{5}\)
\(PQ=\sqrt{(3+5)^2+(-3-1)^2}=\sqrt{80}=4\sqrt{5}\)
Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\times6\sqrt{5}\times4\sqrt{5}=\class{ans}{60}\)		 1A

Question 5 Past Paper 8 marks

The coordinates of the points \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) and \(M\) are \((0,2)\), \((4,0)\), \((9,5)\) and \((5,8)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(P\), \(Q\) and \(R\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). (3 marks)
(b) Prove that \(M\) lies inside \(C\). (2 marks)
(c) Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\). Let \(N\) be a point lying on \(C\) such that it is nearest to \(M\).
(i) Describe the geometric relationship among \(G\), \(M\) and \(N\).
(ii) Find the equation of the straight line which passes through \(M\) and \(N\). (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Let the equation of \(C\) be \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\).
Through \(P(0,2)\): \(4+2E+F=0\quad\cdots(1)\)
Through \(Q(4,0)\): \(16+4D+F=0\quad\cdots(2)\)
Through \(R(9,5)\): \(106+9D+5E+F=0\quad\cdots(3)\)		 1M
From (1): \(F=-4-2E\). Sub into (2): \(4D-2E=-12\Rightarrow 2D-E=-6\quad\cdots(4)\)
Sub \(F\) into (3): \(9D+3E=-102\Rightarrow 3D+E=-34\quad\cdots(5)\)
(4)+(5): \(5D=-40\Rightarrow D=-8\)
\(E=-10\), \(F=16\)		 1M
Equation of \(C\): \(\class{ans}{(x-4)^2+(y-5)^2=25}\) i.e., \(x^2+y^2-8x-10y+16=0\)
Centre \(G=(4,5)\), radius \(r=5\).		 1A
(b) Distance from \(G(4,5)\) to \(M(5,8)\):
\(\sqrt{(5-4)^2+(8-5)^2}=\sqrt{1+9}=\sqrt{10}\approx3.16\)
Since \(\sqrt{10}<5=r\), \(M\) lies inside \(C\). \(\blacksquare\)		 1M, 1A
(c)(i) \(G\), \(M\) and \(N\) are collinear (they lie on the same straight line, with \(N\) being the point on \(C\) on the ray from \(G\) through \(M\)). 1M
(c)(ii) Slope of \(GM=\dfrac{8-5}{5-4}=3\).
Line through \(M(5,8)\) with slope 3:
\(y-8=3(x-5)\Rightarrow\class{ans}{3x-y-7=0}\)		 1M, 1A

Question 6 Past Paper 9 marks

The coordinates of the points \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\) are \((0,0)\), \((-40,0)\) and \((0,30)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). (3 marks)
(b) The straight line \(L_1\) passes through \(B\) and \(G\). \(L_2\) is another straight line which passes through \(O\) and cuts \(L_1\) perpendicularly at \(H\). Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(AP=PH\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).
(i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and the line segment \(AH\). (1 mark)
(ii) Write down the coordinates of \(H\). (1 mark)
(iii) Denote the region bounded by \(\Gamma\) and \(C\) that contains \(G\) by \(S\). Someone claims that the area of \(S\) exceeds \(450\pi\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Let \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\).
Through \(O(0,0)\): \(F=0\).
Through \(A(-40,0)\): \(1600-40D=0\Rightarrow D=40\).
Through \(B(0,30)\): \(900+30E=0\Rightarrow E=-30\).
\(C:\,\class{ans}{(x+20)^2+(y-15)^2=625}\), i.e. \(x^2+y^2+40x-30y=0\).
Centre \(G=(-20,15)\), radius \(r=25\).		 1M, 1M, 1A
(b)(i) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AH\). 1M
(b)(ii) Slope of \(L_1\) through \(B(0,30)\) and \(G(-20,15)\):
\(m_{L_1}=\dfrac{30-15}{0-(-20)}=\dfrac{3}{4}\).
\(L_2\) through \(O\) perpendicular to \(L_1\): slope \(-\tfrac{4}{3}\), so \(L_2: y=-\tfrac{4}{3}x\).
Intersection of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\): \(\tfrac{3}{4}x+30=-\tfrac{4}{3}x\Rightarrow\tfrac{25}{12}x=-30\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac{72}{5}\).
\(y=-\tfrac{4}{3}\cdot(-\tfrac{72}{5})=\tfrac{96}{5}\).
\(\class{ans}{H=\!\left(-\tfrac{72}{5},\,\tfrac{96}{5}\right)}\)		 1A
(b)(iii) \(\Gamma\) is a straight line (perp. bisector of \(AH\)). Find the distance \(d\) from \(G(-20,15)\) to \(\Gamma\).
Slope of \(AH=\tfrac{96/5-0}{-72/5+40}=\tfrac{96}{128}=\tfrac{3}{4}\). Midpoint of \(AH=\!\left(-\tfrac{136}{5},\tfrac{48}{5}\right)\).
\(\Gamma: y-\tfrac{48}{5}=-\tfrac{4}{3}(x+\tfrac{136}{5})\Rightarrow 4x+3y+80=0\).		 1M
Distance from \(G(-20,15)\) to \(\Gamma\):
\(d=\dfrac{|4(-20)+3(15)+80|}{5}=\dfrac{45}{5}=9\)
Area of major circular segment (side containing \(G\)) with chord distance \(d=9\), \(r=25\):
Let \(\cos\alpha=\tfrac{9}{25}\Rightarrow\sin\alpha=\tfrac{4\sqrt{34}}{25}\).
Area of \(S=\pi r^2-(r^2\alpha-r^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha)=625\pi-625\arccos\!\tfrac{9}{25}+36\sqrt{34}\). 		1M
Is \(S>450\pi\)?
\(625\pi-625\arccos\tfrac{9}{25}+36\sqrt{34}>450\pi\)
\(\Leftrightarrow 175\pi+36\sqrt{34}>625\arccos\tfrac{9}{25}\)
\(\text{LHS}\approx175(3.1416)+36(5.831)\approx549.8+209.9=759.7\)
\(\text{RHS}\approx625(1.2014)\approx750.9\)
Since \(759.7>750.9\), the area of \(S>450\pi\). The claim is correct.		 1M, 1A

Question 7 Past Paper 6 marks

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((0,5)\) and \((3,1)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point in the rectangular coordinate plane such that \(AP\perp PB\). The locus of \(P\) lies on a circle \(\Gamma\).

(a) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). (2 marks)
(b) \(Q\) is a moving point on \(\Gamma\). \(C=(0,1)\) is a point in the same plane.
(i) Does \(\Gamma\) pass through \(C\)? Explain your answer.
(ii) When the slope of \(CQ\) is \(\sqrt{3}\), someone claims that \(\angle CAQ\) is greater than \(100°\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Since \(AP\perp PB\), \(P\) lies on the circle with diameter \(AB\).
Centre \(=\) midpoint of \(AB=\!\left(\tfrac{3}{2},3\right)\), radius \(=\tfrac{|AB|}{2}=\tfrac{\sqrt{9+16}}{2}=\tfrac{5}{2}\).
\(\Gamma:\,\class{ans}{\!\left(x-\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^2+(y-3)^2=\tfrac{25}{4}}\), i.e., \(x^2+y^2-3x-6y+5=0\).		 1M, 1A
(b)(i) Sub \(C=(0,1)\) into \(\Gamma\): \(0+1-0-6+5=0\,\checkmark\).
Yes, \(\Gamma\) passes through \(C\). 		1M
(b)(ii) Line \(CQ\): \(y=\sqrt{3}\,x+1\). Intersect with \(\Gamma\):
\(x^2+(\sqrt{3}x+1)^2-3x-6(\sqrt{3}x+1)+5=0\)
\(4x^2-(4\sqrt{3}+3)x=0\Rightarrow x=0\) (point \(C\)) or \(x=\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}+3}{4}\).
\(Q=\!\left(\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}+3}{4},\,\dfrac{16+3\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)\).		 1M
Vectors from \(A(0,5)\):
\(\overrightarrow{AC}=(0,-4)\), \(\overrightarrow{AQ}=\!\left(\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}+3}{4},\,\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}-4}{4}\right)\)
\(\overrightarrow{AC}\cdot\overrightarrow{AQ}=(-4)\cdot\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}-4}{4}=4-3\sqrt{3}\)
\(|\overrightarrow{AQ}|^2=\dfrac{(4\sqrt{3}+3)^2+(3\sqrt{3}-4)^2}{16}=\dfrac{100}{16}\Rightarrow|\overrightarrow{AQ}|=\dfrac{5}{2}\)
\(\cos\angle CAQ=\dfrac{4-3\sqrt{3}}{4\cdot\tfrac{5}{2}}=\dfrac{4-3\sqrt{3}}{10}\approx\dfrac{-1.196}{10}\approx-0.120\) 		1M
Since \(\cos100°\approx-0.174<-0.120=\cos\angle CAQ\) and cosine is decreasing on \([0°,180°]\):
\(\angle CAQ<100°\).
The claim is disagreed.		 1A
Total: 6 marks

Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark

(b) Describe the relationship between \(C_1\) and \(\Gamma\). (1 mark)

(d) Explain why neither horizontal tangent to \(\Gamma\) intersects \(C_1\). (2 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) \(G=(3,1)\), \(r_1=5\). Tangent length from \(P\) to \(C_1\): \(\sqrt{|PG|^2-25}=\dfrac{|PG|}{\sqrt{2}}\).
Squaring: \(|PG|^2-25=\dfrac{|PG|^2}{2}\Rightarrow\dfrac{|PG|^2}{2}=25\Rightarrow|PG|^2=50\).

(c) Horizontal tangents to \(\Gamma\) have the form \(y=c\). Distance from \(G(3,1)\) to \(y=c\) equals \(r_2\):

Question 1 Past Paper 9 marks

The coordinates of the points \(A\) and \(B\) are \((-10,\,0)\) and \((30,\,0)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\). It is given that the \(y\)-coordinate of \(G\) is \(-15\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). (3 marks)
(b) The straight line \(L\) passes through \(B\) and \(G\). Another straight line \(\ell\) is parallel to \(L\). Let \(P\) be a moving point such that the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(L\) equals the perpendicular distance from \(P\) to \(\ell\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that \(\Gamma\) passes through \(A\).
(i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(L\). (1 mark)
(ii) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). (2 marks)
(iii) Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\) at another point \(H\). Someone claims that \(\angle GAH < 70°\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) The \(x\)-coordinate of \(G\) is the midpoint of \(A\) and \(B\):
\(\dfrac{-10+30}{2}=10\), so \(G=(10,-15)\).		 1M
Radius of \(C\):
\(r=\sqrt{(-10-10)^2+(0+15)^2}=\sqrt{400+225}=\sqrt{625}=25\)		 1M
Equation of \(C\): \((x-10)^2+(y+15)^2=625\)
i.e. \(x^2+y^2-20x+30y-300=0\)		 1A
(b)(i) \(\Gamma\) is parallel to \(L\). 1M
(b)(ii) Slope of \(L\) (through \(B(30,0)\) and \(G(10,-15)\)):
\(m_L=\dfrac{-15-0}{10-30}=\dfrac{3}{4}\)
Since \(\Gamma \parallel L\) and \(\Gamma\) passes through \(A(-10,0)\):
\(y-0=\dfrac{3}{4}(x+10)\)		 1M
\(3x-4y+30=0\) 1A or equivalent
(b)(iii) \(\tan\angle ABG=\dfrac{3}{4}\), so \(\angle ABG\approx36.87°\). 1M
Since \(GA=GB=r=25\), \(\triangle GAB\) is isosceles, so \(\angle GAB=\angle GBA=\angle ABG\).
Since \(HA=HB\) (\(H\) lies on \(\Gamma\), the perpendicular bisector of the chord from \(A\) to the circle's axis), we also have \(\angle BAH=\angle ABG\).
\(\angle GAH=\angle GAB+\angle BAH=2\angle ABG\approx73.74°\).		 1M
Since \(\angle ABG>35°\), we have \(\angle GAH>70°\).
The claim is disagreed.		 1A f.t.
Total: 9 marks

A vertical line through \(B\) cuts the line \(y=mx\) at point \(C\), where \(m\) is a positive constant with \(AB=BC\). Let \(D\) be a point such that \(ABCD\) is a square. Express the slope of \(OD\) in terms of \(m\). (3 marks)
(b) The coordinates of \(M\) and \(N\) are \((6,5)\) and \((10,0)\) respectively. Let \(P\) and \(Q\) lie on \(OM\) and \(MN\) respectively, and let \(R\) and \(S\) lie on the \(x\)-axis. If \(PQRS\) is a square, find the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P\). (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Let \(B=(b,0)\). Then \(C=(b,\,mb)\), \(A=(b+mb,\,0)=(b(m+1),0)\), and \(D=(b(m+1),\,mb)\). 1M for any one

\(=\dfrac{m}{m+1}\) 1A

By (a), slope of \(OQ=\dfrac{5/6}{5/6+1}=\dfrac{5/6}{11/6}=\dfrac{5}{11}\).

Equation of line \(MN\) (through \(M(6,5)\) and \(N(10,0)\)):

\(\dfrac{5}{11}x=\dfrac{-5}{4}x+\dfrac{25}{2}\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{22}{3}\)

Question 3 Past Paper 7 marks

The straight line \(L\) cuts the positive \(x\)-axis and the positive \(y\)-axis at points \(M\) and \(N\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point such that \(MP=NP\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\). It is given that the equation of \(\Gamma\) is \(3x-2y-30=0\).

(a) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and \(MN\). (1 mark)
(b) Find the equation of \(L\). (3 marks)
(c) Suppose that \(\Gamma\) cuts \(L\) and the \(x\)-axis at points \(Q\) and \(R\) respectively. Find the area of quadrilateral \(ONQR\), where \(O\) is the origin. (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\). 1M
(b) Let \(M=(a,0)\) and \(N=(0,b)\). Slope of \(L=\dfrac{-b}{a}\).
Since \(\Gamma\perp L\) (by (a)): \(\left(\dfrac{-b}{a}\right)\!\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)=-1\Rightarrow 2a=3b\quad\cdots(1)\)		 1M
Midpoint of \(MN=\!\left(\dfrac{a}{2},\dfrac{b}{2}\right)\) lies on \(\Gamma\):
\(3\!\left(\dfrac{a}{2}\right)-2\!\left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)-30=0\Rightarrow 3a-2b=60\quad\cdots(2)\)
From (1): \(a=\frac{3b}{2}\). Sub into (2): \(\frac{9b}{2}-2b=60\Rightarrow b=24,\;a=36\).		 1M
Equation of \(L\): \(y-0=\dfrac{-24}{36}(x-36)\Rightarrow 2x+3y-72=0\) 1A or equivalent
(c) \(N=(0,24)\). \(R\) is the \(x\)-intercept of \(\Gamma\): set \(y=0\Rightarrow x=10\), so \(R=(10,0)\).
\(Q\) is the intersection of \(\Gamma\) and \(L\): solve \(3x-2y=30\) and \(2x+3y=72\).
Multiplying: \(9x-6y=90\) and \(4x+6y=144\). Adding: \(13x=234\Rightarrow x=18,\;y=12\).
So \(Q=(18,12)\).		 1M
Area of \(ONQR\) (trapezoid with vertices \(O(0,0)\), \(N(0,24)\), \(Q(18,12)\), \(R(10,0)\)):
Split into \(\triangle ONQ\) and \(\triangle OQR\):
\(=\dfrac{(24)(18)}{2}+\dfrac{(10)(12)}{2}=216+60\)		 1M
\(=276\) 1A

Question 4 Past Paper 7 marks

The straight line \(L\) passes through the point \((-10,\,11)\). The slope of \(L\) is \(-2\).

(a) Find the equation of \(L\). (2 marks)
(b) The coordinates of \(H\) and \(K\) are \((2,5)\) and \((-4,-7)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a point on \(L\) such that \(P\) is equidistant from \(H\) and \(K\).
(i) Find the coordinates of \(P\). (2 marks)
(ii) Let \(Q\) be a point such that \(HPKQ\) is a quadrilateral where \(HP=HQ=KQ\). Find the area of quadrilateral \(HPKQ\). (3 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) \(-2=\dfrac{y-11}{x+10}\Rightarrow -2x-20=y-11\Rightarrow 2x+y+9=0\) 1M
\(2x+y+9=0\) 1A
(b)(i) From \(L\): \(y=-2x-9\). Let \(P=(p,\,-2p-9)\).
Setting \(PH=PK\):
\((p-2)^2+(-2p-14)^2=(p+4)^2+(-2p-2)^2\)
Expanding: \(p^2-4p+4+4p^2+56p+196=p^2+8p+16+4p^2+8p+4\)
\(52p+200=16p+20\Rightarrow 36p=-180\Rightarrow p=-5\)		 1M
\(P=(-5,\,-2(-5)-9)=(-5,\,1)\) 1A
(b)(ii) Since \(HQ=KQ\), point \(Q\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(HK\).
Midpoint of \(HK=(-1,-1)\). Slope of \(HK=\dfrac{-7-5}{-4-2}=2\). Slope of perp. bisector \(=-\dfrac{1}{2}\).
Perp. bisector: \(y+1=-\dfrac{1}{2}(x+1)\Rightarrow y=-\dfrac{x}{2}-\dfrac{3}{2}\).		 1M
Setting \(HQ=HP=\sqrt{65}\) with \(Q=(q,\,-q/2-3/2)\):
\((q-2)^2+(-q/2-3/2-5)^2=65\)
\((q-2)^2+(q/2+13/2)^2=65\)
Multiply by 4: \(4(q-2)^2+(q+13)^2=260\)
\(5q^2+10q-75=0\Rightarrow (q+5)(q-3)=0\)
\(q=-5\) (giving \(P\)) or \(q=3\Rightarrow Q=(3,-3)\).		 1M
Since \(HP=PK=KQ=QH=\sqrt{65}\), \(HPKQ\) is a rhombus.
Diagonal \(HK=\sqrt{(2+4)^2+(5+7)^2}=\sqrt{180}=6\sqrt{5}\)
Diagonal \(PQ=\sqrt{(-5-3)^2+(1+3)^2}=\sqrt{80}=4\sqrt{5}\)
Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\times6\sqrt{5}\times4\sqrt{5}\)
\(=60\) 1A

Solution
Working Mark Remark

Through \(Q(4,0)\): \(16+4D+F=0\;\cdots(2)\)
Through \(R(9,5)\): \(106+9D+5E+F=0\;\cdots(3)\)		 1M

(4)+(5): \(5D=-40\Rightarrow D=-8\Rightarrow E=-10\Rightarrow F=16\)		 1M

(c)(i) \(G\), \(M\) and \(N\) are collinear, with \(N\) on segment \(GM\) extended to the circle. 1M
(c)(ii) Slope of \(GM=\dfrac{8-5}{5-4}=3\).

Question 6 Past Paper 9 marks

The coordinates of \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\) are \((0,0)\), \((-40,0)\) and \((0,30)\) respectively. The circle \(C\) passes through \(O\), \(A\) and \(B\). Denote the centre of \(C\) by \(G\).

(a) Find the equation of \(C\). (3 marks)
(b) The straight line \(L_1\) passes through \(B\) and \(G\). The straight line \(L_2\) passes through \(O\) and cuts \(L_1\) perpendicularly at \(H\). Let \(P\) be a moving point such that \(AP=PH\). Denote the locus of \(P\) by \(\Gamma\).
(i) Describe the geometric relationship between \(\Gamma\) and the line segment \(AH\). (1 mark)
(ii) Write down the coordinates of \(H\). (1 mark)
(iii) Denote the region bounded by \(\Gamma\) and \(C\) that contains \(G\) by \(S\). Someone claims that the area of \(S\) exceeds \(450\pi\). Is the claim correct? Explain your answer. (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Let \(x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0\).
Through \(O(0,0)\): \(F=0\).
Through \(A(-40,0)\): \(1600-40D=0\Rightarrow D=40\).
Through \(B(0,30)\): \(900+30E=0\Rightarrow E=-30\).		 1M, 1M
\((x+20)^2+(y-15)^2=625\), i.e. \(x^2+y^2+40x-30y=0\)
Centre \(G=(-20,15)\), radius \(r=25\).		 1A
(b)(i) \(\Gamma\) is the perpendicular bisector of \(AH\). 1A
(b)(ii) Slope of \(L_1\) (through \(B(0,30)\) and \(G(-20,15)\)):
\(m_{L_1}=\dfrac{30-15}{0+20}=\dfrac{3}{4}\).
\(L_2\) through \(O\) perpendicular to \(L_1\): slope \(=-\dfrac{4}{3}\), so \(L_2: y=-\dfrac{4}{3}x\).
Intersection of \(L_1\) and \(L_2\): \(\dfrac{3}{4}x+30=-\dfrac{4}{3}x\Rightarrow \dfrac{25}{12}x=-30\Rightarrow x=-\dfrac{72}{5}\).
\(y=-\dfrac{4}{3}\cdot\!\left(-\dfrac{72}{5}\right)=\dfrac{96}{5}\).
\(H=\!\left(-\dfrac{72}{5},\,\dfrac{96}{5}\right)\) 1A
(b)(iii) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\) (perp. bisector of \(AH\)):
Slope of \(AH=\dfrac{96/5-0}{-72/5+40}=\dfrac{96/5}{128/5}=\dfrac{3}{4}\).
Midpoint of \(AH=\!\left(-\dfrac{136}{5},\,\dfrac{48}{5}\right)\). Slope of \(\Gamma=-\dfrac{4}{3}\).
\(\Gamma: 4x+3y+80=0\).		 1M
Distance from \(G(-20,15)\) to \(\Gamma\):
\(d=\dfrac{|4(-20)+3(15)+80|}{5}=\dfrac{|-80+45+80|}{5}=\dfrac{45}{5}=9\)		 1M
Let \(\theta\) be the half-central angle of the chord (where \(\Gamma\) cuts \(C\)), with \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{9}{25}\), \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{4\sqrt{34}}{25}\).
Area of minor segment \(=r^2\theta - r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta=625\theta-36\sqrt{34}\).
Area of \(S=625\pi-(625\theta-36\sqrt{34})=625\pi-625\theta+36\sqrt{34}\).		 1M
Numerically: \(\theta=\arccos\!\dfrac{9}{25}\approx1.2010\) rad, \(\sqrt{34}\approx5.831\)
Area of \(S\approx625(3.14159)-625(1.2010)+36(5.831)\approx1963.5-750.6+209.9\approx\mathbf{1422.8}\)
\(450\pi\approx450(3.14159)\approx\mathbf{1413.7}\)
Since \(1422.8>1413.7\), the area of \(S>450\pi\). The claim is correct. 1A

Question 7 Past Paper 6 marks

The coordinates of \(A\) and \(B\) are \((0,5)\) and \((3,1)\) respectively. Let \(P\) be a moving point such that \(AP\perp PB\). The locus of \(P\) lies on a circle \(\Gamma\).

(a) Find the equation of \(\Gamma\). (2 marks)
(b) \(Q\) is a moving point on \(\Gamma\). \(C=(0,1)\) is a point in the same plane.
(i) Does \(\Gamma\) pass through \(C\)? Explain your answer.
(ii) When the slope of \(CQ\) is \(\sqrt{3}\), someone claims that \(\angle CAQ\) is greater than \(100°\). Do you agree? Explain your answer. (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Since \(AP\perp PB\), \(P\) lies on the circle with diameter \(AB\).
Centre \(=\) midpoint of \(AB=\!\left(\dfrac{3}{2},3\right)\), radius \(=\dfrac{AB}{2}=\dfrac{\sqrt{9+16}}{2}=\dfrac{5}{2}\).		 1M
\(\Gamma:\left(x-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2+(y-3)^2=\dfrac{25}{4}\), i.e. \(x^2+y^2-3x-6y+5=0\) 1A
(b)(i) Sub \(C=(0,1)\) into \(\Gamma\): \(0+1-0-6+5=0\;\checkmark\).
Yes, \(\Gamma\) passes through \(C\). 		1M
(b)(ii) Line \(CQ\): \(y=\sqrt{3}\,x+1\). Sub into \(\Gamma\):
\(x^2+(\sqrt{3}x+1)^2-3x-6(\sqrt{3}x+1)+5=0\)
\(4x^2-(4\sqrt{3}+3)x=0\Rightarrow x=0\) (point \(C\)) or \(x=\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}+3}{4}\).
\(\therefore Q=\!\left(\dfrac{4\sqrt{3}+3}{4},\,\dfrac{16+3\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)\).		 1M
Since \(A=(0,5)\) and \(C=(0,1)\) both have \(x=0\), line \(AC\) is vertical.
Slope of \(AQ=\dfrac{\frac{16+3\sqrt{3}}{4}-5}{\frac{4\sqrt{3}+3}{4}-0}=\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}-4}{4\sqrt{3}+3}\).
Since \(3\sqrt{3}\approx5.196>4\), slope of \(AQ>0\), so \(Q\) is above and to the right of \(A\).
Since \(AC\) points straight down from \(A\) and \(AQ\) points upper-right from \(A\):
\(\angle CAQ=90°+\arctan\!\left(\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}-4}{4\sqrt{3}+3}\right)\approx90°+\arctan(0.1205)\approx90°+6.9°\approx96.9°\) 		1M
Since \(96.9°<100°\), \(\angle CAQ<100°\). The claim is disagreed. 1A
Total: 6 marks
Question 8 Past Paper 7 marks

Denote the origin by \(O\). The coordinates of \(A\) and \(B\) are \((2t,\,3t)\) and \((5t,\,5t)\) respectively, where \(t\) is a positive constant. Denote the reflection image of \(A\) with respect to \(OB\) by \(C\).

(a) Someone claims that \(OABC\) is a rhombus. Do you agree? Explain your answer. (3 marks)
(b) If the area of \(OABC\) is \(245\), find the coordinates of \(C\). (4 marks)
Working Space
Solution
Working Mark Remark
(a) Slope of \(OB=\dfrac{5t}{5t}=1\), so line \(OB\) has equation \(y=x\).
Reflecting \(A(2t,3t)\) over \(y=x\) swaps the coordinates: \(C=(3t,\,2t)\).		 1M
Slope of \(OA=\dfrac{3t}{2t}=\dfrac{3}{2}\);  Slope of \(CB=\dfrac{5t-2t}{5t-3t}=\dfrac{3t}{2t}=\dfrac{3}{2}\).
Since slope of \(OA=\) slope of \(CB\), we have \(OA\parallel CB\).
Slope of \(OC=\dfrac{2t}{3t}=\dfrac{2}{3}\);  Slope of \(AB=\dfrac{5t-3t}{5t-2t}=\dfrac{2t}{3t}=\dfrac{2}{3}\).
Since slope of \(OC=\) slope of \(AB\), we have \(OC\parallel AB\).
\(\therefore OABC\) is a parallelogram.		 1M
\(OA=\sqrt{(2t)^2+(3t)^2}=t\sqrt{13}\) and \(OC=\sqrt{(3t)^2+(2t)^2}=t\sqrt{13}=OA\).
Since adjacent sides \(OA=OC\), \(OABC\) is a rhombus. Agree.		 1A
(b) Area of \(OABC\) by the shoelace formula with \(O(0,0),A(2t,3t),B(5t,5t),C(3t,2t)\):
\(=\dfrac{1}{2}|0(3t-2t)+2t(5t-0)+5t(2t-3t)+3t(0-5t)|\)
\(=\dfrac{1}{2}|0+10t^2-5t^2-15t^2|\)
\(=\dfrac{1}{2}\times10t^2=5t^2\)		 1M, 1M
\(5t^2=245\Rightarrow t^2=49\Rightarrow t=7\) (since \(t>0\)). 1M
\(C=(3\times7,\,2\times7)=(21,\,14)\) 1A

Creative Question 3 New 7 marks

Solution
Working Mark Remark
